What is Warfarin?

Warfarin is an anticoagulant (often called a blood thinner). It helps prevent harmful blood clots from forming or growing.

It works by interfering with vitamin K–dependent clotting factors. Because it can increase bleeding risk, your dose is individualized and carefully monitored.

Take it exactly as prescribed. Take it at the instructed time each day. Do not stop, change your dose, or double a dose on your own.

Your INR & Monitoring

INR (International Normalized Ratio) is a blood test used to measure how long your blood takes to clot and help guide your warfarin dose.

  • Keep scheduled INR appointments.
  • Your target INR depends on your condition and is set by your clinician.
  • Report missed appointments and follow new dosing instructions carefully.
Remember: Do not adjust warfarin based on an INR result unless your anticoagulation team has instructed you how to do so.

Vitamin K & Your Diet

Vitamin K can reduce warfarin's effect. You do not need to avoid green vegetables! The key is to eat a similar amount of vitamin K-rich foods consistently from week to week.

Foods rich in Vitamin K Spinach, kale, collard greens, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cabbage, and other leafy greens.
What to aim for Keep your usual intake steady. Contact your healthcare team before making a major diet change or starting a diet plan.
Holiday food reminder Enjoy your usual portions and patterns. Avoid suddenly adding large servings of greens or cutting them out.
Check before supplements Vitamin K supplements, multivitamins, nutrition shakes, and herbal products may affect treatment. Ask before starting or stopping.

Everyday Safety Tips

  • Use a soft-bristled toothbrush and an electric razor.
  • Be careful with knives, scissors, and other sharp tools.
  • Avoid activities with a high risk of falls or injury.
  • Tell every doctor, dentist, and pharmacist that you take warfarin.
  • Ask before using OTC medicines—especially aspirin, ibuprofen, or naproxen—or new supplements.
  • Wear or carry medical identification indicating you take warfarin.

Missed Dose?

If you miss a dose, follow your prescriber's instructions or contact your anticoagulation clinic for guidance. Do not take a double dose to make up for a missed one.

Tell your care team if you accidentally take extra warfarin.

Other reminders

  • Ask your clinician about alcohol.
  • Let your care team know about illness, vomiting, diarrhea, or significant changes in eating.
  • If pregnant, planning pregnancy, or possibly pregnant, contact your clinician promptly—warfarin can harm a developing fetus.

When to Get Medical Help

Seek emergency care (call 911) for signs of serious bleeding such as vomiting blood or material resembling coffee grounds, coughing up blood, black/tarry stool, heavy uncontrolled bleeding, severe or unusual headache, fainting, new confusion or weakness, or a fall/head injury—even if you feel okay.
  • Contact your healthcare team promptly for unusual bruising, persistent nosebleeds, bleeding gums, pink/red urine, or bleeding that is not normal for you.
  • Report any fall or injury, especially a head impact.
